Dear Qualcomm Recruiting Team,

I am applying for the Embedded Engineering Internship for Summer 2027. I'm a
Computer Science student at RIT in the combined bachelor's and master's
program, with an expected graduation date of December 2027, and most
of what I do outside of class is embedded firmware for real physical systems.
On the RIT Electric Vehicle Team, I built the CAN/CANopen communication layer
between our motorcycle's VCU, motor controller, power subsystem, and BMS from
scratch, and wrote the C++ firmware running on that VCU. At GE Vernova, I
built a full I/O subsystem from scratch in C on an STM32F7 for industrial
radios, and in my free time I'm building an autonomous fixed-wing flight
controller on an ESP32S3 with FreeRTOS and PID stabilization.

I understand this internship places candidates across several tracks based on
background, so I want to be upfront about where my background points:
automotive. Vehicle systems firmware is already a big part of what I do
outside of class, and Snapdragon Digital Chassis, running the compute stack
behind cockpit, ADAS, and connectivity for automakers worldwide, is exactly
the larger-scale version of that work I want to grow into.

A few specifics: I've written hard real-time firmware in both ThreadX and
FreeRTOS, including a 300\,Hz control loop on the EVT VCU running across dual
CAN buses at the same time as board-level fault handling, which is a small
version of the mixed-criticality problem Snapdragon Ride Flex is solving by
putting cockpit, ADAS, and AI workloads on one chip. I designed the VCU's
CANopen object dictionary and CAN message formats myself. For low-level
interfaces, I have production firmware
experience with SPI, I2C, UART, ADC, and PWM, and I've written device drivers
including a GNSS driver and a custom SPI display driver. I also debug at the
hardware level regularly, using a Saleae logic analyzer, oscilloscope, and
J-Link Segger to trace firmware issues back to root cause rather than
guessing.

I would welcome the opportunity to talk more about where I could contribute.
Thank you for your consideration.
