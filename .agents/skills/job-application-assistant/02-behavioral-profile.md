# Behavioral Profile

## Overview
Travis Brown's 16Personalities result: **ISFJ-T (Defender)** — 84% Introverted, 59% Observant, 57% Feeling, 82% Judging, 54% Turbulent. This pattern describes a methodical, reliable, detail-oriented engineer who follows through on commitments, works best in structured environments, and takes quiet satisfaction in making systems work correctly rather than in external recognition.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Introversion | 84% | Prefers deep focus and fewer, more meaningful interactions; does best with sustained, uninterrupted technical work |
| Observant (practical) | 59% | Pragmatic and grounded; focused on concrete reality and what's actually happening, not abstract speculation |
| Feeling | 57% | Values team harmony and cooperation; approaches collaboration with empathy; avoids unnecessary conflict |
| Judging (structured) | 82% | Highly organized and thorough; plans ahead, follows through, values completing tasks properly over moving fast |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Thorough and reliable:** Follows through on commitments completely; does not leave things half-finished. Strong at documentation, testing, and verification steps that others skip.
- **Detail-oriented under complexity:** Notices things others miss — memory for specifics, careful attention to edge cases. This pairs well with hardware debugging (Saleae, oscilloscope, DMM) where small details matter.
- **Pragmatic and grounded:** Focuses on what actually works in practice, not theoretical ideals. Evaluates tools and approaches based on concrete results.
- **Collaborative and low-ego:** Prefers team harmony; does not need to be the loudest voice in the room. Lets work speak for itself. Cooperative rather than competitive with teammates.

## How You Work Best
- Close to hardware — direct microcontroller and peripheral register access
- Systems where code has real physical consequences: sensors feeding decisions, actuators producing movement (motors, flaps, arms)
- Hands-on bench work: logic analyzer, DMM, oscilloscope — diagnosing problems at the signal and hardware level
- Structured environments with clear expectations, defined responsibilities, and realistic timelines
- In-person or hybrid — benefits from stable team relationships and direct access to hardware
- Projects with real-time constraints and well-defined interfaces (RTOS, protocol specifications)

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Preference for established methods:** Can be deliberate when evaluating new approaches. Frame as: "I evaluate new tools against proven alternatives before adopting them — I prioritize reliability over novelty."
- **Modest self-promotion:** Tends not to claim credit loudly. Frame as: "I let concrete results and shipped work demonstrate capability rather than relying on self-description."
- **Conflict avoidance:** Prefers consensus and may defer in disagreements. Frame as: "I raise technical concerns through evidence and data rather than confrontation, and I seek alignment before proceeding."

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "Detail-oriented", "thorough", "reliable", "follows through"
- "Collaborative", "team player", "cross-functional"
- "Quality-focused", "rigorous testing", "documentation"
- "Structured", "organized", "process-driven"
- Small-to-mid-size teams with clear ownership and defined scope

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker, but worth noting):
- "Move fast and break things", "high ambiguity", "wear many hats" in a chaotic startup context — Travis can handle ambiguity but needs some structure to do his best work
- "Aggressive", "competitive", "high-pressure sales-style culture" — mismatches the collaborative preference
- Fully remote with no team interaction — benefits from in-person or hybrid access to both people and hardware

## Management Style Preferences
- Works best with managers who set clear expectations, give direct feedback, and respect deep-focus time
- Prefers to understand the "why" behind a task, not just the "what"
- Will raise concerns methodically (with evidence) rather than loudly — managers who interpret quiet as agreement may miss this
- Does not need constant check-ins once trust is established, but appreciates clear communication on priorities

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Emphasize reliability, thoroughness, and follow-through alongside technical depth. Mention specific examples of rigorous testing (logic analyzer, bench debugging) to show the hands-on quality focus.
- **CV:** Surface concrete, measurable outcomes (75% QA time reduction, stable multi-day runtime after leak fix) — the Judging/detail trait shows in results, not claims.
- **Interviews:** Use STAR examples that demonstrate completing complex tasks end-to-end, catching problems others missed, or making a system reliable. Avoid overselling — specific, factual answers land better than enthusiasm.
- **Don't overstate:** Don't claim to thrive in pure chaos or love ambiguity. Do frame the preference for structure as a quality/reliability asset, not rigidity.
